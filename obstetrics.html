    Planning a pregnancy

    Getting your body ready

    Preconception and prenatal care help prevent pregnancy complications and catch them early. Start these steps at least three months before you hope to conceive.

    • Schedule a preconception exam and talk with your provider about family health history
    • Avoid smoking and alcohol
    • Limit caffeine
    • Take a prenatal vitamin with at least 800 micrograms of folic acid
    • Exercise regularly, before and during pregnancy
    • Work toward a healthy weight
    • Eat at least five servings of fruits and vegetables a day
    • See your dentist and keep up with flossing
    • Check your employer's policies on appointments and maternity or paternity leave
    • Ask your insurance about coverage for pregnancy, delivery, and newborn care

    Your pregnancy, step by step

    What to expect with us

    After a positive test

    Your first visit

    Visits usually begin after your first missed period or a positive home pregnancy test.

    • An hour or more with a nurse practitioner, including a physical exam and lab work
    • Our nurses talk with you about nutrition, exercise, and a healthy pregnancy
    • Ultrasound scheduled for weeks 9 to 12
    • Our financial services team contacts you about birth and newborn costs
    Weeks 11 to 13, then monthly

    Regular prenatal visits

    Your second visit includes a physical exam, a review of your labs, and a conversation about any prenatal testing you may need. After that, we see you monthly.

    Weeks 32 to 40

    Getting ready

    At 32 weeks we review the signs of labor and fill out your delivery paperwork. From 36 weeks until delivery, you see your doctor every week.

    • Learn car seat use and the required car seat inspection
    • Ask your provider about scheduling a tour of the Maternity Suites
    Delivery day

    The Maternity Suites

    You deliver at Citizens Medical Center with a team that includes your provider, a nurse for you, a nurse for your baby, and a respiratory therapist.

    Medications during pregnancy

    Over-the-counter medicines that are generally safe

    Some medicines are not safe during pregnancy. The ones below are generally considered safe, but check with your provider about your own situation.

    Cold, sinus & allergyTylenol, Tylenol Cold, Tylenol Sinus, Benadryl, Sudafed, Sudafed Sinus, Dristan nasal spray, Ocean nasal spray
    Sore throat & coughChloraseptic spray, Cepacol lozenges, cough drops (any brand), Robitussin
    Heartburn & upset stomachTums, Mylanta, Riopan, Pepcid
    NauseaDramamine, Emetrol
    DiarrheaImodium AD
    ConstipationColace, Milk of Magnesia, Metamucil
    HemorrhoidsAnusol cream, Anusol HC .01%

    Do not take

    Advil, Aleve, aspirin, ibuprofen, Motrin, Excedrin, or other NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs).

    Not sure about a medicine? Call Family Center at (785) 462-6184.

    Packing list

    What to bring to the hospital

    Bring a photo ID and your insurance card to every visit.

    For you

    • Birth plan
    • Birthing gown (we can provide one)
    • Comfortable clothes, socks, and shoes
    • Robe and slippers
    • Glasses, or contacts with case and solution
    • Hair bands, lip balm, lotion, and toiletries
    • Phone and charger
    • Camera or video recorder
    • Books, magazines, music, or games for relaxing

    For your baby

    • A going-home outfit with socks and a soft cap
    • A receiving blanket
    • A properly installed car seat. Your local sheriff's department can help you check the installation.
    • A pacifier, if you want one. We recommend waiting until breastfeeding is well established. As a Baby-Friendly and High 5 for Mom & Baby hospital, we do not provide pacifiers.

    Leave at home

    • Jewelry
    • Large amounts of cash or credit cards
    • Sentimental or irreplaceable items

    Call your provider right away if you notice

    If you need medical attention, call ahead so an obstetrical nurse knows you are on your way.

    Citizens Medical Center(785) 462-7511

    • Swelling of the face, fingers, or body, or sudden weight gain
    • Severe headache, blurred vision, or seeing spots
    • Abdominal pain or vomiting that won't stop
    • Chills, fever, or painful urination
    • Vaginal bleeding, discharge, or leaking fluid
    • A change in how much your baby is moving
    • Signs of preterm labor: a low, dull backache, pelvic pressure, diarrhea, spotting, or watery discharge
    • Any accidental injury

    For a life-threatening emergency, call 911.

    Breastfeeding support

    Baby-Friendly designatedHigh 5 for Mom & Baby

    Breast milk gives your baby a balance of nutrients that is easy to digest, and breastfed babies have a lower risk of illness and infection. Citizens Health is a designated Baby-Friendly hospital, the gold standard for supporting breastfeeding, and is recognized by High 5 for Mom & Baby for following five evidence-based breastfeeding practices.

    Visiting the Maternity Suites

    For family and friends

    These guidelines protect new moms and babies while they rest and recover.

    • 10am to 8:30pm dailyVisits outside these hours are up to the new mom.
    • Quiet time, 2 to 3:30pmOnly one support person and a sibling may visit during quiet time.
    • After deliveryPlease wait about two hours before visiting, unless the mom asks otherwise.
    • ChildrenSiblings of the baby are welcome. Other children under 12 may not visit the unit.
    • If you are sickPlease stay home. Anyone with symptoms of illness will not be allowed to visit.
    • Clean handsWash your hands when you enter and leave the room, and cover coughs and sneezes with your elbow.
